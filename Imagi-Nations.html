The ethos

What your character can do matters. So does how they connect with people. Imagi-Nations gives both a place at the table.

Welcome to Dracouroboros: goblin clans, broken towers and dragons with long memories. The game runs on one friendly die, so a seven-year-old and someone with twenty years of adventures behind them can share the same table. Roll 1d6 and add a stat. A natural six bursts, so you roll again.

At its heart is the SAMHSA framework for trauma-informed practice, built into the rules themselves. You can always ask for support, you keep a say in what happens to your character, and a defeat never pushes you out of the game. The Storyteller doesn’t have to add these things. The mechanics already make room for them.

That gives you room to try, get it wrong and still be part of the story. It’s why the same game works in a classroom, a recovery setting or around the kitchen table.

The ten stats

Six stats for what you do. Four for how you connect.

Capability

What your character can do.

Muscle
Strength, force and vitality. Carries all Health.
Skill
Precision, speed and sure footing. Half of Defense and all Movement.
Tough
Endurance and grit. The other half of Defense.
Smarts
Reasoning, memory and invention.
Senses
Noticing details, tracking and reading a room.
Charm
Presence, performance and personality that draws people in.

Connection

How your character connects, reflects, repairs and speaks up.

Heart
Courage, compassion and standing by someone.
Mind
Reflection, perspective and seeing through deception.
Hands
Care, repair, making and mending.
Voice
Speaking up, asking and setting a boundary.

Resolve — Heart + Mind

Resolve protects your courage, focus and sense of who you are. Fear, grief, awe and coercion are rolled against Resolve. Feeling something is never treated as weakness.

A Bridge Roll brings one Capability stat and one Connection stat together when the outcome depends on both what you do and how you go about it. Talking a frightened child down from a ledge is Voice and Heart together. Steadying a friend’s hand while you reset a broken bone is Smarts and Hands.

With ten stats, every strength has a place. There’s no dump stat and no wrong kind of character. If you’re the quiet player who keeps talking the party out of trouble, your numbers reflect that too.

Expressions — sharing how you feel

Seventeen ways to be heard without speaking.

Sometimes you know how your character feels but can’t quite find the words. Sometimes the scene moves before you can. An Expression gives you a signal to choose. Set it, change it or clear it whenever you like, without spending an action, making a roll or explaining yourself. It has no mechanical effect at all.

Choose one

Choose an Expression and see what it shares with the table.

Each Expression uses a simple statement in your character’s voice. In play, it appears beside their portrait and on their token, so the table can see it at a glance.

An Expression is something your character chooses to show. It doesn’t reveal their private thoughts or prove they’re telling the truth. An Expression never authorises forced behaviour: an Angry character can still act gently, a Withdrawn one can still step in, and an Affectionate one can still say no.

You choose your own character’s Expression. The Storyteller chooses Expressions for creatures, and leaves your hero’s feelings in your hands.

Attitudes — how you approach the moment

Choose your approach. See where it helps and hinders.

An Expression shares how you feel. An Attitude says how you go about things. It also affects the roll: you gain a bonus die when your approach suits the situation, and a penalty die when it doesn’t.

Bold

“I meet this head-on.”

Facing a challenge directly, with courage and decisive action.

HelpsDirectly confronting danger or opposition.

HindersWhen caution, patience, or subtle observation is essential.

When a roll comes up, decide together: this helps, this hinders, or neither. The result records your answer and the clause behind it, so everyone can see why it applies. The people at the table decide what fits the moment.

No approach is better than another. Guarded and Open-Hearted are different ways to meet a moment. So are Hesitant and Bold. Your Attitude describes how your character approaches this situation. It doesn’t judge the person playing them.

Trauma-informed by design

What the SAMHSA principles mean at the table.

Safety

At 0 Health, your hero is Taken Out, not killed. They return with a setback that becomes part of the story. One unlucky roll won’t leave you watching everyone else play for the rest of the session.

Trust & transparency

Every State tells you how it ends. Every roll shows the full sum. When something affects your character, you can see the reason.

Peer support

Help is never rationed. A hint, more time or a teammate’s hand costs nothing. Needing support never uses up a resource.

Collaboration

Help and Power Cards put teamwork into the rules. Supporting each other has numbers behind it.

Voice & choice

You can decline any beat and rejoin it without a penalty. Social rolls can change willingness, access or cost. They cannot force belief, loyalty or confession, or override a boundary you’ve set.

Cultural & identity humility

Ten stats give you ten ways to shine. The game makes room for different kinds of people and strengths, without quietly favouring one.

Where it works

The same game, for very different rooms.

Classrooms

Play gives Storytellers a way to notice and record strengths across ten pillars, including communication, regulation, collaboration and problem-solving. These strengths show up through the adventure, rather than a test. They can be recorded without stopping the story.

Therapeutic & recovery settings

Play gives you space to practise self-regulation, trust, asking for help and the courage to be seen trying. Expressions let you signal distress without having to perform it.

Kitchen tables

One die. Ten stats. Goblin clans, broken towers and a story to share. Bring different ages and levels of experience to the same table, with maths everyone can follow.
